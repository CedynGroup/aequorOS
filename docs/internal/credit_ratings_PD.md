# Implied Credit Rating & Probability of Default for Banks: A Defensible Methodology for AequorOS

## TL;DR
- **Build a transparent, CAMELS-anchored scorecard that mirrors the published Moody's/S&P/Fitch bank frameworks, calibrate it to an ordinal rating scale now, and treat the PD as an explicitly uncertain, conservatively-bounded quantity — not a precise number — because single-market African banks are a textbook "low-default portfolio" (LDP) where naive default-frequency estimation is statistically indefensible.**
- **The central technical obstacle is the low-default problem: use the Pluto–Tasche most-prudent (upper-confidence-bound) estimator, Bayesian priors, and external benchmark/pooling anchored to agency default studies, and apply an explicit EBA-style Margin of Conservatism — with the Ghanaian sovereign (which defaulted in 2022 and forced the DDEP that impaired 22 banks by GH¢37.7bn) acting as a hard ceiling and dominant systematic risk factor.**
- **Stage the build: (1) a defensible, versioned, expert-anchored scorecard producing an indicative rating + PD band for internal benchmarking and early warning; (2) statistical recalibration as data accumulates; govern the whole thing under SR 11-7 with discrimination (AUC/Gini), calibration (binomial/Hosmer–Lemeshow/traffic-light) and stability (PSI) testing. Repo haircuts, limits, and eligibility gates remain disabled until the calibrated methodology has independently passed the release gate in the implementation specification.**

**Release gate for money-moving use.** Stage-1 ratings may inform private monitoring immediately, but they must not set live repo haircuts, counterparty limits, eligibility, or on-chain parameters until AequorOS has approved calibration evidence, an independent validation record, a named model owner, and the required data-quality sign-off. The authoritative gate is `AequorOS_Implied_Rating_PD_Implementation.md` §15.

## Key Findings

**1. Banks are scored differently from corporates.** The supervisory lingua franca is CAMELS (Capital, Asset quality, Management, Earnings, Liquidity, Sensitivity to market risk), a 1–5 scale system run by US regulators. Rating agencies operationalize the same economics into standalone credit assessments: Moody's Baseline Credit Assessment (BCA), S&P's Stand-Alone Credit Profile (SACP), and Fitch's Viability Rating (VR). All three separate the bank's intrinsic financial strength from external (sovereign/parent) support, then re-combine them — a structure AequorOS should copy exactly.

**2. Implied ratings from financials are an established discipline.** Moody's RiskCalc (private-firm EDF from financial statements), S&P Capital IQ's fundamental PD model, and Bloomberg DRSK all map financial ratios to a PD and then to a rating scale. The dominant statistical engine is logistic/probit regression on transformed accounting ratios; the market-based Merton/KMV distance-to-default approach is more accurate but requires traded equity, which mid-tier Ghanaian banks lack.

**3. The low-default-portfolio (LDP) problem is the binding constraint.** A single emerging market yields almost no bank defaults, so observed default frequencies are ~0 and statistically meaningless. The canonical solution is Pluto–Tasche's most-prudent estimation (upper confidence bound subject to monotonic rating ordering), complemented by Bayesian methods, external benchmarking/pooling, shadow ratings, and a regulatory Margin of Conservatism (MoC).

**4. Ghana's sovereign risk dominates.** Ghana defaulted on external debt in 2022 and executed a Domestic Debt Exchange Programme (DDEP) that inflicted large NPV losses on banks holding government paper — a direct demonstration that a Ghanaian bank's PD is inseparable from sovereign risk. The sovereign should function as a ceiling and as the dominant systematic factor in the correlation structure.

**5. Defensibility requires SR 11-7-grade governance.** Because outputs feed repo/collateral decisions, the model must be documented for conceptual soundness, independently validated, back-tested, and monitored — with discrimination, calibration and stability metrics, and honest confidence bounds.

## Details

### 1. Bank-specific scoring frameworks and their actual ratios

**CAMELS.** Developed by the FFIEC in 1979 as the Uniform Financial Institutions Rating System (UFIRS); the sixth component, Sensitivity to market risk, was added in 1997. Each component is scored 1 (best) to 5 (worst); ratings are confidential supervisory outputs. Typical public-data proxies per component:
- **Capital adequacy:** CET1 ratio, Tier 1 leverage ratio, total capital ratio (CAR), tangible common equity/tangible assets.
- **Asset quality:** NPL ratio (non-performing/gross loans), loan-loss reserves/NPLs, provisioning coverage, loan concentration/growth.
- **Management:** largely qualitative; proxied by efficiency (cost-to-income) ratio and growth discipline.
- **Earnings:** ROA, ROE, net interest margin (NIM), earnings quality/volatility.
- **Liquidity:** loan-to-deposit ratio, deposit composition (stable vs. wholesale/brokered), LCR, NSFR.
- **Sensitivity:** interest-rate repricing/duration gap, FX exposure, trading book size.

Academic work confirms CAMELS ratios have predictive power for bank distress; the OCC's own research (Gaul et al.) finds CAMELS composite and Management ratings predict future ROA, NPLs, and stock returns.

**Moody's Baseline Credit Assessment (BCA).** The BCA is Moody's measure of standalone default probability absent support, expressed on a lowercase 21-notch alphanumeric scale (aaa down to c). Structure confirmed from Moody's Banks Methodology (2021, updated Nov 2024 and Nov 2025):
- **Macro Profile:** a 15-point overlay (Very Strong+ to Very Weak–) built from banking country risk plus a Credit Conditions factor; it positions the score each financial ratio maps to (the same ratio scores higher in a stronger system). Shown as "Weighted Macro Profile … 100%."
- **Financial Profile → Solvency (3 sub-factors):** Asset Risk = problem loans / gross loans (problem loans = impaired loans, or non-accrual plus accruing loans 90+ days past due); Capital = (tangible common equity − intangibles) / risk-weighted assets (RWA); Profitability = net income / tangible assets (ROTA).
- **Financial Profile → Liquidity (2 sub-factors):** Funding Structure = market funds / tangible banking assets (market funds exclude subordinated debt); Liquid Resources = liquid banking assets / tangible banking assets. (The Nov 2025 update relabeled these to "Less-stable Funds (Non-LCR)/TBA" and "Core Banking Liquidity (Non-HQLA)/TBA.")
- Sub-factors combine through grids into a Combined Solvency Score and Combined Liquidity Score, then a Financial Profile score. **Moody's does not publish fixed numeric percentage weights** for these sub-factors — combination is grid/matrix-based, with Solvency weighted more heavily than Liquidity and Asset Risk/Capital more than Profitability. Any circulating "25%/25%/12.5%" figures are unofficial reconstructions.
- **Qualitative adjustments** (notching): Business/Geographic Diversification, Opacity & Complexity, Strategy/Risk Appetite & Governance, plus a sovereign/affiliate constraint. The scorecard yields a three-notch range from which the committee sets the assigned BCA.
- Then Affiliate Support → Adjusted BCA → Loss Given Failure (LGF) notching → Government Support → instrument ratings.

For inputs Moody's uses "the weaker of the three-year average and the latest annual figure" for problem-loan and profitability ratios, and the latest figure for capital — a conservative, anti-cherry-picking convention AequorOS should adopt.

**S&P SACP.** The Issuer Credit Rating is built from the Stand-Alone Credit Profile plus support. The SACP starts from an **anchor** derived from the Banking Industry Country Risk Assessment (BICRA) — a combination of economic risk and industry risk scored 1–10, mapping to an anchor ranging from 'a' (least risky) to 'b-'. The anchor is then notched up/down by four bank-specific factors: **Business Position** (business stability, concentration/diversity, management & strategy), **Capital & Earnings** (primarily the risk-adjusted capital [RAC] ratio, plus a capital sustainability ratio and earnings buffer ratio), **Risk Position** (growth, concentrations, complexity), and **Funding & Liquidity** (funding ratios and liquidity metrics; a bank with "weak" liquidity or an "at risk" regulatory ratio is capped). System support is embedded in the anchor; extraordinary support is added later.

**Fitch Viability Rating.** The all-in Issuer Default Rating (IDR) is the higher of the VR (intrinsic strength) or the support-driven rating (Government Support Rating / Shareholder Support Rating) — a "higher of" approach. The VR uses an Operating Environment score (anchored to a Country Ceiling) and Key Rating Drivers scored on the 'aaa' scale via two-factor matrices, with published core metrics and standard weights (Fitch Criteria Essentials – Bank Ratings):
- **Business Profile** — total operating income (20%)
- **Asset Quality** — impaired loans / gross loans (20%)
- **Earnings & Profitability** — operating profit / risk-weighted assets (15%)
- **Capitalisation & Leverage** — core capital ratio (25%, most heavily weighted)
- **Funding & Liquidity** — loans / customer deposits (20%)
- **Risk Profile** — qualitative attributes (10%)
These produce an Implied VR that analytical judgment adjusts. Fitch's published two-factor matrices (metric 1 = operating-environment score; metric 2 = the financial ratio) are the single most directly copyable template for a transparent AequorOS scorecard.

Both BIS surveys and academic work confirm all three agencies share the intrinsic-strength-plus-support architecture and translate letter grades to numbers (AAA=20 … C=0) for analysis.

### 2. Implied / statistical rating models

**Moody's RiskCalc** predicts private-firm EDF (Expected Default Frequency) from financial statements. It uses a small set of ratios (to avoid overfitting) grouped as Profitability (NI/assets, interest coverage), Capital Structure/Leverage (retained earnings/assets, liabilities/assets), Liquidity, Activity, and Size (assets), and — critically — the modeling pipeline: (i) non-parametric transforms of each ratio into interim PDs (capturing non-linearity), (ii) a probit/logit combination with industry factors, (iii) a final non-parametric transform mapping the score to an actual EDF calibrated to observed default rates, with a distance-to-default-based Credit Cycle Adjustment. RiskCalc explicitly **excludes financial institutions** — a warning that generic corporate models cannot be applied to banks. It can be run in "Financial-Statement-Only" (FSO) mode, which is exactly AequorOS's data situation.

**Bloomberg DRSK** is a hybrid: a Merton distance-to-default core augmented with additional statistically significant fundamental factors, fed through a logistic regression to real-world PDs and a 1-year credit-risk scale (an internal letter grade). Per Bondioli, Goldberg, Hu, Li, Maalaoui & Stein, "The Bloomberg Corporate Default Risk Model (DRSK) for Public Firms" (SSRN 3911300, 2021), the updated public-firm model's "accuracy ratio is above 92%, [and] adjusted pseudo R-squareds have improved." There is a separate DRSK private-firm model using financial statements plus distance-to-default proxies.

**S&P Capital IQ** offers fundamentals-based PD (PD Model Fundamentals) and market-signal PD (Market Signals, a Merton variant), plus CreditModel (a statistical quantitative-scoring tool mapping financials to a letter-scale credit score). The pattern across all vendors is identical: transform ratios → statistical combination → PD → map PD to an ordinal scale via a master scale.

**Which ratios matter most for financial institutions:** capital/leverage (TCE/RWA, leverage ratio), asset quality (problem loans/gross loans, provisioning), profitability (ROTA/ROA, NIM), funding/liquidity (loan/deposit, market-funds reliance, liquid assets), and size. Region-specific evidence from Opoku, Appiah & Gyimah, "Can We Predict the Financial Distress of Banks in Sub-Saharan Africa?" (SAGE Open 2024, DOI 10.1177/21582440241274127; 228 listed and non-listed SSA banks, 2006–2016) finds that "capacity (cost to income), capital (leverage), and condition (loan loss reserves to gross loan and inflation) positively affect the financial distress" of SSA banks, and that "control of corruption decreases the probability of financial distress."

### 3. PD modeling methods and calibration

**Accounting-based / reduced-form statistical models:**
- **Logistic regression scorecards** — the industry workhorse; outputs a probability directly, interpretable coefficients, regulator-familiar.
- **Altman Z-score family** — the emerging-market variant is Z'' = 3.25 + 6.56·X1 + 3.26·X2 + 6.72·X3 + 1.05·X4 (X1=working capital/total assets, X2=retained earnings/TA, X3=EBIT/TA, X4=book equity/total liabilities), with the +3.25 constant standardizing a zero score to a D-rated (default) bond; Z''>2.6 "safe," 1.1–2.6 "grey," <1.1 "distress." Altman explicitly designed this for non-manufacturers and emerging markets where a country-specific model cannot be built for lack of data — directly relevant to Ghana. Note: the classic Z-score was built on manufacturers and maps poorly onto bank balance sheets, so it is a benchmarking cross-check, not a core bank engine.
- **Shumway (2001) discrete-time hazard model** ("Forecasting Bankruptcy More Accurately: A Simple Hazard Model," *Journal of Business* 74(1):101–124) — argues single-period models are biased/inconsistent; a hazard model (estimable as a logit on firm-years) uses time-varying covariates and the full panel. Finds about half the classic accounting ratios are insignificant once market variables (size, past returns, return volatility) are added. This is the correct statistical form for AequorOS's panel of bank-years.
- **Discriminant analysis** — Altman's original technique; largely superseded by logit/hazard.

**Structural / market-based models:** the Merton (1974) model treats equity as a call option on assets; default occurs when asset value falls below debt at horizon. Distance-to-default DD = (ln(V/D) + (μ − ½σ²)T) / (σ√T); PD = N(−DD). Moody's KMV replaces the normal CDF with an empirically calibrated EDF (e.g., a DD of ~3.3 historically defaulting at ~0.4% vs. the theoretical ~0.04%). **These require market equity and equity volatility, which private mid-tier Ghanaian banks do not have** — so structural models can only be used indirectly (e.g., a listed-peer or sovereign DD as a systematic overlay), not as the primary engine.

**Calibration** is the step that turns an ordinal score into an actual PD:
- Map model score → rating grade → PD via a **master scale** (a fixed ladder of grades each with an assigned PD, e.g., mirroring agency idealized default rates).
- Anchor grade PDs to a **central tendency** — the long-run average portfolio default rate.
- **PIT vs TTC:** Point-in-Time PD reflects current conditions (aligns with observed default rates now, needed for IFRS 9 ECL); Through-the-Cycle PD is a long-run average smoothed over the cycle (used for capital, less procyclical). A model is typically hybrid; both calibrations may be required. Given Ghana's volatility, AequorOS should produce a PIT view for live repo decisions and a TTC view for limit-setting, and be explicit about which is which.

### 4. The low-default-portfolio problem (the crux)

With few or zero defaults, the naive estimator (defaults/obligors ≈ 0) has no conservatism and is statistically meaningless. Established solutions:

**Pluto–Tasche most-prudent estimation** (Pluto & Tasche, 2005, "Estimating Probabilities of Default for Low Default Portfolios"). The principle: assume the ordinal ranking is correct (pA ≤ pB ≤ pC), then estimate each grade's PD as the **upper confidence bound** consistent with the observed (near-zero) defaults, under the most-prudent assumption that a grade's PD equals that of all worse grades pooled. For zero defaults, solve 1−γ ≤ (1−p)^n for p, giving p̂ = 1 − (1−γ)^(1/n). Their Table 1 worked example (nA=100, nB=400, nC=300, zero defaults observed) gives upper-bound p̂A = 0.09% / 0.17% / 0.29% / 0.37% / 0.57% / 0.86% at confidence levels γ = 50% / 75% / 90% / 95% / 99% / 99.9% respectively — smaller samples and higher confidence give higher (more conservative) PDs; the authors note "values of γ smaller than 95% seem more appropriate." The method extends to a few defaults (binomial tail), to correlated defaults via a one-factor Vasicek model, to multi-period data, and can be scaled to a portfolio central tendency. This is the single most defensible technique for AequorOS's zero-default reality and should be the PD floor.

**Bayesian approaches** (Kiefer; Tasche) place a prior (expert-informed or uninformative/uniform) on PD and update with sparse data, yielding a full posterior distribution — naturally producing confidence bounds and dispensing with an arbitrary confidence level (at the cost of prior subjectivity). Quasi-Moment-Matching (Tasche) and CAP-curve/Van der Burgt calibration are related industry techniques, all scaled to a common central tendency for comparability.

**External benchmarking and pooling:** because internal Ghanaian default data are essentially absent, anchor to (i) rating-agency idealized/observed default rates by grade (the master scale), (ii) pooled regional/emerging-market bank default and distress data, and (iii) shadow ratings — replicating agency ratings where they exist and extending statistically to unrated banks. This is standard LDP practice.

**Regulatory expectations:** Basel II ¶451 requires that "estimates of PDs … likely to involve unpredictable errors" carry "a margin of conservatism that is related to the likely range of errors. Where methods and data are less satisfactory and the likely range of errors is larger, the margin of conservatism must be larger." The EBA Guidelines on PD/LGD estimation (EBA/GL/2017-16) formalize the **Margin of Conservatism (MoC)** framework, classifying deficiencies (data, methodology, general estimation error) and requiring an explicit add-on; the ECB TRIM guide requires the MoC be based on the distribution of the PD estimator. A practical implementation is the **k-sigma method** (MoC = k × standard deviation of the estimator). Basel also imposes a **PD floor of 0.03%** for most exposures and defines default as 90 days past due or unlikeliness to pay — AequorOS should hard-code both.

### 5. Emerging-market and sovereign considerations for Ghana

**Sovereign ceiling / support.** Historically banks were rarely rated above their sovereign; agencies now allow limited exceptions but the sovereign remains a powerful anchor. Moody's caps many bank obligations at the sovereign bank-deposit ceiling; S&P embeds system support via the BICRA anchor; Fitch anchors the VR to the Operating Environment/Country Ceiling. For a Ghanaian bank, the sovereign rating (deeply sub-investment-grade after the 2022 default) should cap the implied issuer rating and heavily weight the operating-environment/macro overlay.

**Sovereign–bank nexus made concrete by the DDEP.** Ghana defaulted on external debt in 2022 and launched the Domestic Debt Exchange Programme (DDEP) on 5 December 2022. Per Finance Minister Ofori-Atta's 4 December 2022 announcement, new bonds' "annual coupon will be set at 0% in 2023, 5% in 2024 and 10% from 2025 until maturity"; the restructured bonds carried an average coupon of 9.1% (down from 19.1%) and an average maturity of 8.3 years (up from 13.8 years) per the Atuahene, Frimpong & Agyei autopsy report. The programme initially targeted GH¢137.3bn (revised to GH¢109.84bn) and ultimately exchanged over GH¢87bn (~US$8bn) of domestic debt, with roughly 85% of eligible bondholders participating (Ghana Ministry of Finance close announcement, 14 February 2023). Using a 16% discount rate, the same autopsy report finds "the DDEP losses of 22 banks stood at GH¢37.7billion, with the private domestic banks and state-owned banks accounting for losses of GH¢19.9billion while foreign-owned banks accounted for GH¢17.8billion"; the IMF (Country Report 23/168) put the average NPV reduction at roughly 30%. These losses "technically rendered some Ghanaian local banks insolvent," forcing recapitalization (partly via the Ghana Financial Stability Fund). This is a live case study that a bank's asset quality, capital, and PD are inseparably tied to sovereign actions — sovereign holdings (usually treated as risk-free) became the primary loss driver.

**Correlation between bank and sovereign risk** must be modeled explicitly: treat the sovereign as the dominant systematic factor in a Vasicek/one-factor structure (raising asset correlation), stress bank capital for sovereign-exposure haircuts, and never treat domestic government securities as risk-free in the Ghanaian context.

**Emerging-market bank credit assessment** differs from developed markets in: weaker/volatile operating environments, thin/absent market data, weaker disclosure and governance, higher inflation and interest-rate volatility, and concentrated loan books. Academic literature specific to the region — bank distress prediction for Sub-Saharan Africa (Opoku et al. 2024), Cameroon CAMEL(S) models, Arab-region and Jamaican CAMELS-based logit early-warning systems, and the classic Demirgüç-Kunt & Detragiache banking-crisis determinants — collectively validate a CAMELS-based logit as the appropriate regional workhorse and highlight macro variables (inflation, corruption control) as material.

### 6. Governance, validation, and defensibility

**SR 11-7 (Fed/OCC, 2011)** is the global benchmark for model risk management. It defines a model broadly (quantitative method processing inputs into estimates, including expert-judgment-based ones), and requires validation across three elements: (1) **conceptual soundness** (design, theory, variable selection, developmental evidence), (2) **ongoing monitoring** (correct implementation, continued performance, process verification, benchmarking), and (3) **outcomes analysis** (back-testing predictions vs. actuals). It mandates independence ("effective challenge"), the three-lines-of-defense governance model, full documentation, and treatment of vendor/third-party models. Basel IRB validation (BCBS) and EBA/PRA (SS1/23) impose parallel expectations.

**Validation metrics AequorOS must implement:**
- **Discrimination** (rank-ordering): Accuracy Ratio / Gini, and AUC/ROC (AUC>0.7 acceptable, >0.8 strong). These are the best-behaved metrics and work even with few defaults if some ranking signal exists.
- **Calibration** (are predicted PDs right in level): binomial test per grade, Hosmer–Lemeshow chi-square across grades, Spiegelhalter/normal test, and the **traffic-light approach** (green/amber/red by deviation of observed from predicted). Caveat: these assume default independence and are weak in LDPs — note their limited power honestly.
- **Stability:** Population Stability Index (PSI) on score distributions and characteristic stability on inputs.
- **Backtesting** over time as defaults/distress events accrue.

**Pitfalls and failure modes to document:** overfitting on tiny samples; false precision (reporting a point PD when only a wide band is supportable); look-ahead/survivorship bias in the bank panel; accounting non-comparability (IFRS vs local GAAP, restated DDEP losses); window dressing of period-end ratios; ignoring sovereign correlation (the DDEP failure mode); stale data; and calibration drift in a high-inflation regime. The honest posture — indicative rating + explicit PD confidence interval + MoC — is itself a defensibility feature.

### 7. Practical implementation for AequorOS

**Architecture: transparent scorecard, not black box.** Given (a) the LDP data reality, (b) the need to defend outputs to regulators and a tokenized-repo counterparty, and (c) the founder's SR 11-7 background, a glass-box scorecard is strongly preferred over ML. It is explainable, auditable, stable on small samples, and maps naturally to agency methodologies. Reserve ML/gradient-boosting as a challenger/benchmark model only.

**Recommended design:**
1. **Component scorecard mirroring CAMELS + agency structure.** Six components; within each, 2–4 ratios AequorOS already computes (CAR, CET1, leverage, LCR, NSFR, NPL/asset quality, ROA/ROE, cost-to-income, loan/deposit, market-funds reliance). Score each ratio via monotonic transforms to a common sub-score scale (RiskCalc-style), using agency-style two-factor matrices (Fitch's template) where the second axis is a Ghana operating-environment/macro score.
2. **Standalone score → apply sovereign ceiling and support** exactly as agencies do (intrinsic + support), with Ghana's sovereign as cap and dominant macro overlay.
3. **Map to an ordinal master scale** (e.g., a 17–21 grade lowercase scale aligned to agency notches) — deliver the ordinal rating as the primary, most-defensible output.
4. **Attach a PD band, not a point.** Calibrate grade PDs via Pluto–Tasche upper confidence bounds (zero/few-default form) anchored to external/agency central tendencies, apply an explicit EBA-style MoC (k-sigma), enforce the 0.03% Basel floor, and publish a confidence interval. Label outputs "indicative" until internal data justify tightening.
5. **Governed methodology object:** versioned parameters (weights, thresholds, transforms, master-scale PDs, MoC, confidence level) stored with lineage; every rating reproducible from a parameter version + input vintage; change control and a model-validation report per SR 11-7.

**Staged roadmap:**
- **Stage 1 (now): Defensible expert-anchored scorecard.** Ship the CAMELS/agency scorecard producing an indicative rating + conservative PD band. Calibrate via shadow ratings (replicate any agency-rated Ghanaian/regional banks) + Pluto–Tasche + regional benchmarks + MoC. This is fully defensible today despite zero internal defaults.
- **Stage 2 (12–36 months): statistical recalibration.** As AequorOS accumulates a panel of bank-years and distress/near-miss events (regulatory breaches, DDEP-type impairments, recapitalizations as default proxies), fit a discrete-time hazard/logit (Shumway form), re-estimate transforms and weights, tighten confidence bounds, refine PIT/TTC calibration, and stand up the challenger ML benchmark.
- **Stage 3 (mature): full IRB-grade PD.** Once defaults/distress events are statistically sufficient, calibrate to observed central tendencies, run full discrimination/calibration/stability backtesting, and narrow the MoC.

**Connection to repo collateral and counterparty limits.** The implied rating and PD band should drive: (i) **haircuts** on repo collateral and on the counterparty's own credit (higher PD → higher haircut; use the upper PD bound, not the mid, for haircut sizing so uncertainty is priced conservatively); (ii) **counterparty exposure limits** (limit tiers by rating grade); (iii) **eligibility gates** (below a grade/above a PD ⇒ ineligible or punitive terms); and (iv) **wrong-way-risk controls** given sovereign–bank correlation (concentration limits on banks with heavy sovereign exposure, since a Ghana sovereign event hits collateral value and counterparty PD simultaneously). For the tokenized-repo partner, the versioned methodology and reproducible lineage are what make on-chain haircut/limit parameters auditable and defensible.

## Recommendations

1. **Build the transparent CAMELS-plus-agency scorecard first (Stage 1).** Copy Fitch's two-factor-matrix VR template (Capitalisation & Leverage 25%, Asset Quality 20%, Funding & Liquidity 20%, Business Profile 20%, Earnings 15%, Risk Profile 10%) and Moody's Solvency/Liquidity ratio definitions (problem loans/gross loans; TCE/RWA; NI/tangible assets; market funds/TBA; liquid assets/TBA). Deliver an ordinal indicative rating as the headline output. *Threshold to advance:* methodology documented and independently reviewed for conceptual soundness.

2. **Treat PD as a bounded, uncertain quantity from day one.** Implement Pluto–Tasche upper-confidence-bound PDs (zero- and few-default forms) at a stated γ below 95%, an explicit k-sigma MoC, the 0.03% Basel floor, and a published confidence interval. Never expose a false-precision point PD for a zero-default portfolio. *Change trigger:* only narrow bands when discrimination/calibration tests on accumulated data justify it.

3. **Hard-wire the sovereign.** Apply Ghana's sovereign rating as a ceiling, use it as the dominant systematic factor (elevated asset correlation in the Vasicek overlay), stop treating domestic government securities as risk-free, and stress every bank for a DDEP-style sovereign shock (calibrated to the observed ~30% average NPV loss). *Threshold:* any bank whose capital cannot absorb a modeled sovereign haircut is flagged/ineligible.

4. **Adopt SR 11-7 governance now, not later.** Versioned parameters + data lineage (already an AequorOS strength), reproducible ratings, independent validation, and the three-lines model. Report Gini/AUC for discrimination and binomial/Hosmer–Lemeshow/traffic-light for calibration, with PSI for stability — while honestly stating LDP limits on calibration tests.

5. **Stage toward a calibrated PD as data accrue (Stages 2–3).** Capture distress/near-miss events as default proxies, fit a Shumway hazard/logit, add PIT (for live repo pricing) and TTC (for limits) calibrations, and keep an ML challenger for benchmarking only. *Threshold to Stage 3:* sufficient default/distress observations for statistically meaningful central-tendency calibration.

6. **Prepare outputs for conservative repo mechanics, then activate only after validation.** The eventual integration sizes haircuts and limits off the upper PD bound, gates eligibility by grade, and enforces sovereign-concentration/wrong-way-risk limits. Do not activate it until the release gate above is satisfied; then expose the versioned methodology to the tokenized-repo partner for on-chain auditability.

## Caveats

- **Moody's does not publish fixed numeric sub-factor weights** for its bank scorecard; the "25%/25%/12.5%" style figures sometimes cited are unofficial reconstructions. Fitch's 25/20/20/20/15/10 weights are published in its Criteria Essentials and are the more citable template. Agency methodologies also change (Moody's updated its bank liquidity ratios in November 2025).
- **Structural (Merton/KMV) models are largely unavailable** to AequorOS because mid-tier Ghanaian banks lack traded equity; they can serve only as an indirect systematic overlay via listed peers or the sovereign.
- **Calibration tests (binomial, Hosmer–Lemeshow) have low power in low-default portfolios** and assume default independence; results must be interpreted cautiously and supplemented with expert/benchmark review.
- **"Default" is ambiguous for banks in a support regime** — regulatory intervention, forbearance, or a DDEP-style restructuring may be the relevant credit event rather than legal bankruptcy. AequorOS must define its credit event explicitly (recommend: 90-days-past-due OR unlikeliness-to-pay OR distressed restructuring/recapitalization) and document it.
- **Some Ghana-specific figures rely on secondary sources** (the GH¢37.7bn DDEP bank-loss figure and the coupon/maturity terms come from the Atuahene, Frimpong & Agyei autopsy report in the Business & Financial Times and from Ministry of Finance announcements, with the ~30% average NPV reduction from IMF Country Report 23/168); primary Bank of Ghana confirmation is advisable before these numbers drive live parameters.
- The build depends on **consistent, comparable financial data** across banks and time; IFRS-vs-local-GAAP differences, DDEP restatements, and period-end window dressing are material risks the ingestion/lineage layer must control.
